\begin{abstract}
This report presents a comprehensive experimental evaluation of NMIGOD on 30 UCI benchmark datasets. Six algorithms are compared under semi-supervised setting. NMIGOD achieves the highest average F1-score (__NM_AVG_F1__) and AUC (__NM_AVG_AUC__). Wilcoxon tests confirm significant superiority over NIEOD (AUC $p<0.001$, F1 $p=$__NM_P_F1__$) and marginal significance over GCN (AUC $p=$__NM_P_AUC__$). NMIGOD ranks first on numerical data. After excluding five challenging datasets, NMIGOD significantly outperforms GCN ($p=$__NM_P_FILT__$).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
NMIGOD addresses anomaly detection in mixed-attribute data through: (1) adaptive neighborhood radius $\varepsilon_a = \sigma_a/(1+\rho_a)$, (2) NMI graph encoding structural similarity, and (3) two-layer GCN for semi-supervised scoring. We compare against ADFNR, DASOD, GCN, GCN-LOF, and NIEOD on 30 UCI benchmarks.

\section{Discussion}
NMIGOD's NMI graph measures structural similarity rather than point-wise distance, weakening spurious outlier-normal connections. The entropy-adaptive radius calibrates neighborhood scale per attribute. On datasets with extremely low anomaly ratios, NMI signal is insufficient.

\section{Conclusion}
NMIGOD achieves highest average F1 (__NM_AVG_F1__) and AUC (__NM_AVG_AUC__) among 6 algorithms. Statistical tests confirm significant superiority over NIEOD and marginal over GCN. NMIGOD ranks first on numerical data. Results validate NMI-graph construction and adaptive radius for mixed-attribute anomaly detection.
